## Resumo disponível

Artificial intelligence companion chatbots are no longer a niche curiosity. Millions of people worldwide now interact daily with conversational agents designed to simulate friendship, romantic partnership, or therapeutic support. While these tools may ease loneliness for some users, a subset develops patterns of emotional dependence that mirror features of behavioural addiction — escalating use, withdrawal-like distress when the service is unavailable, and progressive displacement of human relationships. Mental health nurses are increasingly encountering such presentations, yet no validated nursing assessment framework exists for identifying and responding to AI-chatbot dependence. This research describes the development of a four-domain nursing assessment and care framework through a mixed-methods design. Phase 1 gathered qualitative data from semi-structured interviews with 22 mental health nurses and 14 self-identified heavy chatbot users recruited through online communities in the Netherlands. Thematic analysis generated candidate assessment domains. Phase 2 converted those domains into a structured assessment tool and pilot-tested it with 84 community mental health patients who reported regular chatbot use. A moderate positive correlation was found between daily chatbot usage hours and emotional dependence scores (r = 0.61, p < 0.001). The resulting framework comprises four interconnected domains: screening and identification, psychosocial impact mapping, intervention planning, and monitoring and follow-up. Internal consistency of the 28-item tool was acceptable (Cronbach's alpha = 0.83). The framework offers psychiatric nurses a structured pathway for what is currently an ad-hoc clinical response to a rapidly growing phenomenon.
