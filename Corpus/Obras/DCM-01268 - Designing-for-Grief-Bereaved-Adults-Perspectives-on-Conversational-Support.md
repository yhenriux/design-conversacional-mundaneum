## Resumo disponível

Bereavement support is often difficult to access or poorly matched to what people need, while existing digital interventions tend to rely on structured or asynchronous support rather than responding to grief in the moment. Although conversational agents are beginning to enter this space, little is known about how bereaved people understand their possible role, limits, and design requirements. We report a formative interview study with eight bereaved adults examining experiences of grief and support after loss, and perceptions of conversational grief support. Participants valued conversational agents for reducing social cost, enabling repeated disclosure, and providing on-demand availability, but raised concerns about generic responses, over-validation, escalation, and trust. We derive six implications for designing conversational agents for grief support.
