## Resumo disponível

Conversational search is the realization of an engaging and iterative approach to information retrieval (IR) which allows users to seek information in a multi-turn setting using a natural language. One of the practical applications of conversational search is conversational question answering (ConvQA) in intelligent dialogue systems. Due to the recent advancements and rise in the popularity of digital assistants such as Alexa, Cortana, Siri, Google Assistant, and AliMe, businesses, and organizations are shifting their information-seeking activities to conversational interfaces. One of the significant aspects when designing conversational agents is the effective incorporation of the context to help the model understand the question better and address the user’s information need.This dissertation investigates and addresses the challenges pertinent to effective context incorporation in ConvQA. Before the irrelevant context is filtered out, the first task is to complete and understand the ambiguous follow-up questions. The approach adopted to address this challenge is the generation of contextual and question entities and utilizing those to fill in the missing information gaps. Once the incomplete questions are completed, the next step is to filter out the irrelevant context that doesn’t contribute much to answering the question. Furthermore, adding the entire context brings the noise into the model. To address this challenge, the effect of selecting and adding the turns that match the current question has been analyzed. This matching is performed using a similarity matrix to identify the common features between the current question and the conversational turns. The idea of generating and capturing entities is utilized to filter out the context that is not relevant to the current question. This is termed as ‘hard history selection’. We further improved the model by performing a re-ranking of the selected turns based on their significance to answer the question as a part of ‘soft history selection’ process. The last step is the addition of a term classification layer to highlight the key terms as a clue to the model to better understand the question. Finally, we extended the task of ConvQA to an open-domain setting called, open-domain ConvQA (OD-ConvQA) to bring the task closer to the real-world scenario and improve the scope of conversational search.The experimental results demonstrate that the history selection and modeling approaches proposed in this dissertation can effectively improve the performance of ConvQA models in different settings. The proposed models are compared with the stateof- the-art vis-à-vis different conversational datasets and provide new insights into conversational information retrieval.
