## Resumo disponível

Consumer trust and purchase behaviour in the e-commerce chatbot context have been shaped by anthropomorphism. However, the mechanism through which anthropomorphic chatbot features influence consumer response remains underexplored. Drawing on Social Presence Theory and the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), a quantitative survey was conducted with 517 Indian online consumers which indicated that anthropomorphic features in chatbots strengthen trust and purchase intention, with social presence and perceived authenticity playing an important mediating role. Emotional contagion was found not to have a significant role. Further, the anthropomorphism-trust relationship was moderated by perceived chatbot intelligence and trust-purchase intention link was moderated by perceived risk. This study contributes to the existing theory by measuring the combined effect of various factors which form trust and lead to purchase intention. The findings extend theoretical understanding by decomposing the socio-emotional and rational pathways through which anthropomorphism shapes consumer behaviour and offers practical insights for designing credible, authentic and competence-driven chatbots that foster trust and encourage purchase intentions in digital commerce.
