## Resumo disponível

The current literature highlights a shortage of investment in mental health services and a growing demand for such resources in North America which is a crucial shortcoming.Despite these needs, there continue to be geographical and financial barriers to accessing mental health care which has left clinicians overburdened.With the rise in the use of artificial intelligence (AI)-chatbots and advancements in voice analytics in clinical care, voice-based AI-powered (voice AI) chatbots emerge as a promising tool to alleviate this scarcity.Leveraging the use of voice as a biomarker for clinical support, voice AI has been anticipated to provide an easy and cost-effective means of collecting health data while increasing accuracy in diagnosis, predicting, and monitoring interventions.Vocal biomarkers coupled with the 24/7 availability, individualized support, and remote application provided by AI-powered chatbots, voice AI chatbots have the potential to improve therapeutic care.However, the design and usage of such chatbots for mental health can bring about a myriad of ethical, legal, and social implications (ESLIs), such as providing inadequate support and guidance due to bias in the design, lack of data protection and privacy regulations, and overreliance on the technology itself.Thus, the need to develop ethical guidelines for oversight is imperative.By taking on an anticipatory ethics approach, this project uses a 2-round Delphi process to identify the ELSIs at each stage of a medical AI lifecycle in the advent of such a technology.A panel of experts from varying disciplines along with community representatives were tasked to 'voice' their opinions on the values they believe should be central to such a technology's design and usage.The results will help explore what constitutes trustworthy voice AI chatbots and to what extent can therapeutic tasks be replaced by AI.By involving key stakeholders in the early stages of supporting ethical guidelines for voice-based mental health chatbots, the project's results will help avoid ELSIs in the design and development of these technologies downstream when deployed in therapeutic settings.
