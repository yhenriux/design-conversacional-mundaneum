## Resumo disponível

The growing prevalence of social media in the Global South in both urban and rural communities presents an opportunity to design technology for teacher communities in low-infrastructure contexts. However, engaging rural teachers poses challenges due to limited internet access and low digital literacy, hindering their conventional participation in social media. To address this, we propose an innovative chatbot design that boosts interaction among rural teacher communities. Over four weeks, we deployed a chatbot on Facebook Messenger among 127 teachers in rural Côte d’Ivoire. We then interviewed 17 teachers to uncover that the chatbot encouraged asynchronous participation by addressing questions and facilitating community discussion. Our qualitative findings demonstrate that the chatbot and its accompanying Facebook page enhanced interaction with community members, fostering a virtual community for teachers. These outcomes suggest promising design opportunities for incorporating chatbots into virtual communities for rural teachers in low-infrastructure settings.
