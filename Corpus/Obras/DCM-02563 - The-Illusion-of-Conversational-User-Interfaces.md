## Resumo disponível

Conversational user interfaces are, as the name implies, routinely described as conversational. This label feels intuitive, even perhaps inevitable. But are we truly engaged in conversation with these systems? We argue that we are not, and that stating so is actively misleading. What we call conversation is interaction with systems optimized to generate the most likely response and to simulate understanding without assuming the risks that make conversation meaningful. If these interfaces were genuinely conversational, they would sometimes refuse or disagree, taking accountability; misunderstand, exposing vulnerability; or incur consequence, demonstrating commitment. Our reluctance to design for such properties may reflect current technical constraints, but it also invites a more uncomfortable provocation: perhaps we do not actually want conversational user interfaces, but rather only its illusion.
