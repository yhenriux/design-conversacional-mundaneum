## Resumo disponível

AI chatbots are increasingly embedded in enterprise software, promising easier access to insights through natural language. However, adoption of these chatbots is not guaranteed. Research on chatbot adoption has focused on general and consumer settings, leaving gaps in specialized domains. This thesis investigates what factors influence the adoption of AI chatbots embedded in procurement analytics software. The research was conducted as a qualitative case study, including a literature review and an empirical interview study. The literature review synthesized the general factors that influence AI chatbot adoption into a revised eight-category framework. The empirical study consisted of nine semi-structured interviews with eleven participants: customers of the case company and employees in customer-facing roles. The empirical study identified 39 factors that affected adoption. The factors that mattered most were whether the chatbot met users' expectations, whether its answers were perceived to be accurate, whether users came to trust it, and how well it fit the tasks they needed to do. Adoption was also influenced by the customer organization, through conditions such as management support and innovation culture. The chatbot was adopted where it did something that neither users' existing workflows nor their own general-purpose AI tools could already do, such as complex analysis of the procurement data held in the platform. The most important conclusion is that perceptions formed through initial use of AI chatbots impact adoption critically, and trust lost in early use can be hard to win back.
