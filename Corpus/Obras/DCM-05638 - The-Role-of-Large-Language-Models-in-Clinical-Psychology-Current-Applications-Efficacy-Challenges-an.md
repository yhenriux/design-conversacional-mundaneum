## Resumo disponível

Large Language Models (LLMs) are rapidly entering the mental health landscape, driven significantly by a persistent global gap in access to traditional care. Millions of individuals are already utilizing consumer-grade AI chatbots like ChatGPT for emotional support, motivated by factors such as immediate availability, affordability, and perceived anonymity.1 Early research, including the first randomized controlled trial (RCT) of a generative AI therapy chatbot (Therabot), indicates potential for LLMs to deliver structured therapies like Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) and achieve short-term symptom reductions in mild-to-moderate depression and anxiety, with outcomes and user-reported therapeutic alliance comparable to human therapy benchmarks.2 However, this potential is tempered by significant limitations and risks. Evidence for long-term effectiveness is lacking 4, and current LLMs struggle with complex clinical presentations, severe mental illness, and nuanced therapeutic modalities beyond structured approaches.6 Critical ethical concerns dominate the discourse, including the risk of harmful or inaccurate advice, failures in crisis management highlighted by tragic real-world incidents 8, a lack of regulatory clarity and accountability 8, significant data privacy and confidentiality vulnerabilities 12, and the potential for algorithmic bias to perpetuate health inequities.14 Professional bodies like the American Psychological Association (APA) and British Psychological Society (BPS) urge caution, emphasizing the need for rigorous validation, transparency, clinician involvement, and human oversight.16 The emerging consensus points towards hybrid models, where AI tools augment human clinicians by handling specific tasks like psychoeducation, skills practice, or administrative support, rather than replacing them.12 Responsible innovation necessitates proactive governance, robust safety protocols, continued research into efficacy and safety across diverse populations, and a steadfast commitment to human-centered care.
