## Resumo disponível

Background: Large language model (LLM)-based chatbots are increasingly considered for older-adult companionship, but basic conversational availability does not necessarily support sustained and meaningful interaction. Aim: This thesis studies how an internal company chatbot originally designed for lightweight companionship with Chinese older adults was reworked through life-review-oriented conversational design. Method: The study is an exploratory qualitative case study with a constructive design component. It analyses original companionship interactions, refined life-review sessions, prompt and script materials, oral-history notes, generated narrative materials, and two prototype states from a real product project in which the author served as lead product manager. The corpus involves nine older adult participants. Results: The original companionship mode supported brief, low-friction contact but often produced shallow follow-up and weak continuity when participants moved toward autobiographical meaning. The refined prompting and interaction structure were associated with richer memory entry, longer connected narration, and material that could be organized into memoir-oriented output. The analysis produced user needs and design requirements concerning concrete autobiographical cues, continuity and progress visibility, meaning-preserving follow-up, editable memoir output, and older-adult-oriented voice-first interaction. Conclusions: The thesis does not claim clinical effectiveness or experimental proof of superiority. Its contribution is a requirement- and artefact-oriented account of how AI companionship can be redesigned around life review in a real software engineering product context.
