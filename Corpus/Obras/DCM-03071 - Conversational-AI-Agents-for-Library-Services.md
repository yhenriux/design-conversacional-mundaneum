## Resumo disponível

This study employs a systematic review of research assessing use of conversational Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools in libraries. Following PRISMA 2020 guidelines, systematic literature search was conducted using the Scopus, Web of Science and OpenAlex databases to identify studies on conversational AI tools in libraries. A total of 536 records retrieve, while excluding duplicates and ineligible studies resulting in 26 studies being included for final synthesis. The findings shows integration of conversational AI tools like chatbots, virtual assistants, and generative AI systems such as ChatGPT in academic, public, and special libraries to actively support information retrieval, reference services, FAQs, and user engagement. Using Cochrane’s ROBINS-I framework, risk of bias was assessed and shows low to moderate influences. The adoption of such AI tools offer benefits such as enhanced accessibility, 24/7 service readiness, and improved user satisfaction. While the challenges including ethical and privacy concerns, inadequate infrastructure, limited staff training, and low levels of AI literacy constraints its implementation. The study emphasizes the user-centric integration of conversational AI agents in libraries highlighting the need for capacity- building initiatives, ethical frameworks, and context-sensitive implementation strategies.
