## Resumo disponível

This study investigates the role of generative AI (GenAI) chatbots in supporting students' ill-structured problem-solving.A GenAI chatbot, Probot, was developed to offer adaptive scaffolding through question prompts during problem representation, solution generation and selection, justification, and monitoring and evaluation.Sixty-one undergraduate students in Korea were divided into control and experimental groups.The experimental group solved an ill-structured problem with Probot while the control group used a non-adaptive chatbot.Interaction patterns with GenAI chatbots were analyzed using Epistemic Network Analysis.Results showed the experimental group had strong connections with orientation in problem analysis, solution analysis, and solution elaboration, suggesting that Probot supported students' analytical and elaborative thinking.The control group showed strong connections between problem analysis, solution analysis, and solution suggestions, indicating a focus on information and idea-seeking.The findings highlight Probot's potential in guiding ill-structured problem-solving and offer design insights for adaptive scaffolding.Land, 2004).Given that ill-structured problem-solving demands extensive cognitive and metacognitive skills, scaffolding can be valuable for students who encounter difficulties in the ill-structured problem-solving process.Prior studies on the mechanism to support students in solving ill-structured problems have primarily employed fixed conceptual and metacognitive scaffolding.Such scaffolding is provided to help students who struggle to identify relevant information due to the lack of necessary conceptual knowledge (Kim & Lim, 2019) and to effectively utilize metacognitive skills in solving ill-structured problems (Ge & Land, 2004).For instance, Kim and Lim (2019) provided scaffolding by posting exploratory questions, hints, and visual materials on a bulletin board for solving instructional design problems.Ouyang et al. (2021) offered students voice-and textbased scaffolding through an online platform to solve the practical challenges instructors face in remote online education during COVID-19.Some studies have explored the use of adaptive scaffolding in ill-structured problem-solving.For instance, Winkler et al. (2021) implemented adaptive scaffolding by providing feedback through a personal AI assistant based on students' solutions to legal problems, while Zheng et al. (2022) offered adaptive scaffolding tailored to students' performance in solving C programming problems.However, since adaptive scaffolding messages are often pre-designed by experts or instructors, such scaffolding is limited in its ability to fully address individual students' needs.Additionally, these scaffolding approaches are typically confined to specific domains, such as instructional design, legal, or programming problems.This highlights the need for further research into adaptive scaffolding mechanisms that dynamically respond to students' specific questions or interactions, rather than relying solely on pre-designed messages or narrowly focused problem domains.Generative AI (GenAI), which leverages deep learning models to create diverse content such as images and text, offers significant potential for dynamically generating scaffolding customized based on the context of problems and student input (Lim et al., 2023).In this study, we developed a GenAI-integrated chatbot, Probot, to provide adaptive conceptual and metacognitive scaffolding to support students in solving ill-structured problems.This research investigates how students interact with Probot throughout the problem-solving process.University students who participated in the study were divided into two groups: one with adaptive scaffolding provided by Probot and the other without adaptive scaffolding.Specifically, the study addresses the following research questions: (a) What patterns emerge when students interact with Probot during the ill-structured problem-solving process?(b) How do these interaction patterns differ between the groups with and without adaptive scaffolding?
