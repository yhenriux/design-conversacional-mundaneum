## Resumo disponível

The quest for truly socially intelligent machines marks a significant frontier in Artificial Intelligence (AI) research. As AI systems become increasingly integrated into daily human life, the demand for nuanced, empathetic, and context-aware interactions has grown exponentially. Current AI systems, while advanced in specific tasks, often fall short in understanding and responding to the complex, implicit social cues that underpin natural human communication. This survey provides a comprehensive overview of the emerging landscape of Vision-Language-Large Language Model (VL-LLM) methods, which are rapidly bridging this gap by integrating capabilities from computer vision, natural language processing, and advanced generative AI. We delineate foundational concepts of social intelligence in AI, explore diverse VL-LLM architectural paradigms designed to process multi-modal inputs, and showcase a broad spectrum of applications, from empathetic conversational agents and personalized educational tutors to advanced assistive robotics and intelligent transportation systems. Furthermore, we critically examine the pressing technical, ethical, and societal challenges inherent in developing such sophisticated AI, including issues of bias, privacy, interpretability, and the implications of human augmentation. We conclude by outlining promising future research directions to foster the development of robust, trustworthy, and genuinely socially intelligent machines, aiming to inform and inspire further advancements in human-AI collaboration.
