## Resumo disponível

The use of conversational artificial intelligence (AI) in healthcare has been on the rise, especially through chatbots designed to help patients find health information. Artificial intelligence (AI) conversational chatbots designed for patient use are becoming more widely available, but evidence of their use and effectiveness for potential patients remains limited. This study aims to assess the usability, effectiveness, and reliability of AI health chatbots in providing medical advice, assessing symptoms, and offering personalized health recommendations to Nigerian patients. The phenomenological research design was adopted for this study. Using a purposive sample, data were collected from the lived experiences of 10 participants who have engaged with AI healthcare chatbots for various health concerns from two hospitals in Anambra state: Divine Hospital and Maternity Awka (Private Hospital) and Chukwuemeka Odumegwu Ojukwu Teaching Hospital (Government hospital). In-depth interviews were conducted to capture the participants' personal experiences. Data were analyzed using thematic analysis, focusing on identifying the essence of patients' experiences, reliability, challenges, and the cultural factors influencing their use of conversational AI healthcare chatbots. We found that patients expressed mixed feelings about the reliability of chatbot-generated health information and highlighted its accessibility and convenience, especially in areas with limited healthcare access. The study concludes that while AI chatbots offer a promising solution to improve access to health information in Nigeria, their adoption depends on addressing concerns about trust and accuracy. Based on these findings, we offer insights for developing more user-friendly, culturally appropriate chatbot technologies in the healthcare sector.
