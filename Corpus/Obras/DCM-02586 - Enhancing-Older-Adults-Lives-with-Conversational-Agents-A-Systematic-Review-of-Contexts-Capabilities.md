## Resumo disponível

This systematic review focuses on conversational agents (CAs) for older adults in the context of rapid advances in natural language processing and large language model technologies, as well as the growing elderly population. Conversational AI research for older adults is expanding as the potential of conversational agents to reduce loneliness, improve healthcare, and assist with cognitive screening is increasingly recognized. Research on CAs has explored how they can be designed, developed, and evaluated based on the life circumstances and needs of older adults. We evaluated and screened 163 articles from seven sources and analyzed 30 of them in depth to answer four key research questions. The findings show that these CAs solutions have a positive impact on several aspects of older people’s lives. However, in some cases, user needs still need to be further translated into the functional design of CAs. This review categorizes related research into four key areas: application scenarios (Health & Medical, Daily Life, Competency Improvement, and Psychology & Cognition), functionality and design strategies, user needs, and design evaluation methods. It highlights the multidimensionality and context-dependency of user requirements, emphasizes the importance of Context in design, and suggests future research directions including context-specific investigations, multimodal recognition, continuous learning, social network integration, and interdisciplinary collaboration. Ultimately, this review provides a comprehensive account of CAs for older adults, providing guidance for future research and design.
