## Resumo disponível

With the advancement of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) technology, balancing contextual adaptability with interactive coherence has become a significant challenge in designing intelligent cultural guide systems. Adopting Human-Centered AI (HCAI) as its core design principle, this study develops an interactive cultural guide system that combines the RAG mechanism with an LLM, implemented within the context of the Hakka EcoMuseum. To construct a multi-source knowledge base, the system integrates three heterogeneous knowledge sources—real-time web information, local cultural documents, and User-Generated Content. In the system’s process flow, after a user submits a natural language query via a web interface, an LLM module automatically determines the query’s semantics and classifies it into one of three predefined scenarios: Real-time Attraction Information Query (Scenario 1), Historical and Cultural Guidance Question (Scenario 2), or User Experience and UGC-Based Response (Scenario 3). The system then invokes the corresponding data source based on the scenario type to generate a response, ensuring contextual appropriateness and knowledge traceability. For the empirical evaluation, representative test questions were designed to compare the performance of our proposed system against three mainstream models: ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity.ai. To validate the system’s performance, this study employed both ANOVA and SUS analyses. The ANOVA results revealed significant differences among systems in tasks involving cultural interpretation and UGC-based responses, confirming the RAG-LLM model’s superior ability to handle semantic reasoning and cultural contextualization. In contrast, the real-time attraction information scenario showed no significant difference, consistent with its dependence on factual web data. Complementary SUS testing with participants demonstrated high usability and consistent satisfaction across user groups, supporting the system’s practicality and human-centered design. The research findings validate that the synergistic operation of structured, multi-source corpora and a RAG-LLM mechanism can effectively enhance a system’s understanding of cultural context, content accuracy, and interaction fluency. This outcome provides a valuable reference for future work in automated corpus processing, multimodal information integration, and cross-cultural research and aids in the digital presentation of EcoMuseum content, thereby improving the interactive quality and user experience of cultural sites.
