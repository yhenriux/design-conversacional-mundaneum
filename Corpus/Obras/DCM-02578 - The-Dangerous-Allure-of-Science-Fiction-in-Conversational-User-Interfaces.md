## Resumo disponível

This paper examines the dangerous allure of science fiction within Conversational User Interface (CUI) research, highlighting how anthropomorphism blurs the distinction between scientific reality and speculative fantasy. The authors argue that because speech inherently communicates agency and emotion, users naturally ascribe human-like motivation to CUIs. However, this often results from a suspension of belief similar to theatrical performance rather than actual social agency. The text critiques current research methodologies, such as the Godspeed questionnaire, for failing to distinguish between functional interaction design and theatrical design. To address these issues, the paper proposes a structured framework for researchers to explicitly categorize the fictions employed in their work, including experimental, interactional, theatrical, and future fictions. By clearly demarcating these imaginative elements, the author suggests the field can avoid baseless hype fostering a more rigorous, accountable scientific discourse.
