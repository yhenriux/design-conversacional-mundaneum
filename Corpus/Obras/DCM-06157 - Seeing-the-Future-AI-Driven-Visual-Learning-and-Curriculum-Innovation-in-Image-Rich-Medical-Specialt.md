## Resumo disponível

Artificial intelligence (AI) has rapidly emerged as a transformative force in medical education, particularly in image-rich specialties such as dermatology, radiology, and pathology. These disciplines rely heavily on visual pattern recognition, yet medical curricula often provide insufficient exposure for trainees to develop these skills. AI offers scalable, adaptive, and cost-effective solutions through three primary roles: direct teaching via intelligent tutoring systems, collaborative teaching through natural language processing–enabled chatbots, and empowerment of multiple learners using virtual patient simulations. These tools can enhance diagnostic accuracy, procedural skills, and clinical reasoning while providing personalized feedback and broadening exposure to rare and diverse presentations, including underrepresented skin tones. Advances in generative AI allow the creation of photorealistic clinical images to supplement training datasets, reduce bias, and preserve patient privacy. Despite these benefits, risks include the perpetuation of bias from unrepresentative datasets, overreliance on algorithmic outputs, and limited access to large, high-quality multimodal data. Ethical integration requires diverse dataset development, transparency, bias mitigation strategies, faculty training, and preservation of human oversight. Future directions include incorporating AI into formal curricula, leveraging federated learning to enable collaborative dataset expansion, and conducting longitudinal studies to assess real-world impact on clinical competence and patient outcomes. With thoughtful implementation, AI can augment, not replace, educators, enhance visual diagnostic training, and improve equity in access to high-quality image-rich specialty education.
