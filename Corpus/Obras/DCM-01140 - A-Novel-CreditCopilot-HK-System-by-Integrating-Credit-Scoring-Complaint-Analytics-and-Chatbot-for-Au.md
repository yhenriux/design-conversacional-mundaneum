## Resumo disponível

Automated credit scoring, complaint analytics and chatbot explanation are often developed as separate banking workflows, leaving applicants, analysts, and governance reviewers with fragmented evidence. This paper presents CreditCopilot-HK, a scoped methodology demonstration for auditable credit-risk decision support. The system integrates structured credit records, complaint narratives, and policy documents through reusable evidence artefacts rather than record-level joining. The primary contribution is to operationalise counterfactual explanation as a conversational UX: applicant-level counterfactuals are translated into natural-language improvement guidance so that the chatbot can explain not only why a model estimates elevated risk, but also what actionable improvement directions may reduce model-estimated risk. Empirically, CFPB complaint narratives are used for product–issue taxonomy construction, topic discovery, sentiment classification, and similar-case retrieval; the Taiwan Default dataset is used for probability-of-default modelling, SHAP explanation, fairness screening, SMOTE sensitivity analysis, and counterfactual generation; and HKMA policy documents are used for retrieval-grounded governance responses. Results show an interpretable complaint taxonomy, coherent semantic complaint workflows, a FinBERT sentiment benchmark, and XGBoost risk explanations in which repayment behaviour and exposure dominate the SHAP drivers. The dashboard and chatbot consume the same saved artefacts, demonstrating evidence-layer integration for transparent communication and governance review in a scoped prototype setting.
