## Resumo disponível

The rapid advancement of artificial intelligence (AI) has paved the way for more efficient and accessible e-government services. Conversational agents (aka chatbots), powered by large language models (LLMs), offer a promising solution for enhancing citizen engagement, streamlining bureaucratic processes, and providing instant access to governmental information. This paper presents a knowledge management system of curated Greek governmental data, accessible via a conversational agent, designed to assist internal civil servants in navigating public services, legal frameworks, and administrative procedures. The conversational agent is implemented using a hybrid approach combining a Retrieval-And-augmented-generation (RAG) approach and a fine-tuned LLM, which combines an external document retrieval system, enabling real-time access to up-to-date governmental sources while mitigating the risks of outdated or hallucinated responses. This knowledge management system captures Greek governmental processes from two sources: (a) data available in the Greek MITOS central database, and (b) the Government Gazette, which is the official publication of the Hellenic Republic. The documents from the second source are used on the RAG approach to enhance its knowledge and reliability, while the data from the first source were used for the preparation of a synthetic dataset for the answer generation part of the agent to fine-tune a pre-trained language model, ensuring a deep understanding of policy documents, official guidelines, and public administration protocols. This paper explores both implementations’ architectural differences, performance benchmarks, and practical implications. We analyze their strengths and limitations regarding accuracy, scalability, and adaptability to evolving governmental policies. Our findings contribute to the ongoing discourse on AI-driven public service automation and offer insights for future deployments of AI in e-government ecosystems.
