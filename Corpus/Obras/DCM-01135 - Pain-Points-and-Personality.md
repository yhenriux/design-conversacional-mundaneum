## Resumo disponível

“Think of Alexa as this big invisible box. And within it, you have to discover, with a flashlight, all these specific skills that it has…There’s a lack of discoverability when it comes to what it can do because you can’t look at a screen”. This comment, from a user experience (UX) designer working on voice assistants, captured a universal theme in my interviews regarding the tension between the ethical tenants of UX and the challenges of working on conversation design. System transparency is one of the core principles of usable systems according to Nielsen’s canonical heuristics. But what does it mean for a “user interface” without a screen to be transparent? How do users come to understand what is possible for voice assistants? These questions are of increasing relevance from both a practical and theoretical perspective as assistants like Alexa, Google Home, and Siri permeate our lives. In this chapter, I will address the ways that anthropologists working in conversational UX design are engaging in collaborative projects to apply their knowledge of interpersonal dynamics to artificial intelligence and argue that new technologies need a social science perspective.
