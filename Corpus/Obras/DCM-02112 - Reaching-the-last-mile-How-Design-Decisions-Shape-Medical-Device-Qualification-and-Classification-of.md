## Resumo disponível

Few AI chatbots have transitioned the ’last mile’ from research prototypes to clinical practice, partly because mapping conversational design decisions to regulatory requirements is complicated. This paper presents a design-oriented regulatory analysis of a chatbot supporting stroke survivors. Using Design Space Analysis operationalised through the QOC (Questions, Options, Criteria) framework, HCI researchers and legal scholars assess how variations in three conversational functions — health Q&A, recovery monitoring, and motivating medical adherence — influence qualification and classification under the European Medical Device Regulation (MDR), General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), and AI Act (AIA). Our findings show that seemingly minor design decisions — such as role, actionability, and proactivity — influence regulatory obligations. The paper contributes insights into ten conversational design considerations influencing regulatory interpretation and demonstrates how QOC can support integrating legal considerations into human-centred design workflows. Finally, we suggest compliance-aware pathways to market, while reflecting on whether all chatbots should pursue the ’last mile’.
