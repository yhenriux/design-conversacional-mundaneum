## Resumo disponível

Conversational artificial intelligence has evolved significantly in recent years and is becoming more and more integrated into everyday life. Apart from the various functionalities that these systems provide, a key advantage is that they allow interactions in natural language. Most existing conversational agents are designed for one-to-one interactions, yet real-world conversations frequently involve groups working together through discussion and negotiation to achieve shared tasks, especially in workplaces and social environments. This mismatch limits the effectiveness of conversational agents in collaborative settings. Multi-user conversations differ fundamentally from one-to-one interactions, as each user operates with distinct roles, goals, and preferences. These factors introduce interactional dynamics that challenge the assumptions and design principles of traditional single-user conversational systems. To enable conversational agents to assist effectively and naturally in such environments, it seems necessary to consider a range of conversational characteristics, including identifying and distinguishing between speakers and addressees, managing turn-taking decisions of the dialogue system, maintaining the context of the conversation, resolving ambiguities in user input, and dynamically adapting their dialogue behaviour to suit user expectations. This thesis is thus investigating the requirements for cooperative multi-user interaction in conversational agents by addressing two main research questions. The first research question examines how dialogue management strategies can be redesigned to support the complexities of multi-user interactions by extending foundational concepts traditionally applied in single-user systems. To this end, different approaches for single-user dialogue management strategies are explored, and their applicability in multi-user scenarios is assessed. Given the existing differences between single-user and multi-user interactions, this thesis proposes adaptations to dialogue management that address the specific demands of multi-user scenarios. As part of this effort, the Multi-User Conversation Model is developed to capture the dynamically changing states within group conversations. Moreover, multi-user dialogue management strategies are defined, including dialogue acts for moderation, negotiation, and recommendation behaviour of the conversational agent. The second research question investigates how cooperative interaction can be effectively established between multiple users and a single conversational agent. To explore this, an agent prototype based on the Multi-User Conversation Model and its dialogue management strategies is implemented and evaluated through a series of user studies. To assess the generalisability of the proposed framework and agent prototype across different conversational use-cases, the system was initially deployed in a Human-Robot Interaction scenario using a humanoid robot, and subsequently adapted to a text-based chatbot environment. Four main user studies were conducted to understand how users perceived different system behaviours and to support further system improvements. These studies examined factors within the context of group conversations, such as usability, perceived fairness, user satisfaction, and conversational efficiency. This work advances the state-of-the-art through two primary contributions. The first contribution is an innovative method for modelling support for decision-making in groups. To achieve this, the Multi-User Conversation Model is implemented and applied in different use-cases. This model serves as a theoretical framework that introduces a robust architecture for multi-user conversational agents, capturing key aspects of interaction throughout the course of a conversation. The model is composed of three interconnected state levels that are continuously updated at each step of the interaction: individual user states which reflect personal goals and preferences, a group state containing information on the shared task as well as the overall conversation context, and a dialogue state which tracks and maintains the dialogue flow. The applicability of the model was validated through user studies, which also highlighted an significant impact of the turn-taking strategy of the system on user perception. The second contribution is the design, implementation, and empirical evaluation of novel dialogue management strategies to enable effective and fair interaction in multiuser conversations. Based on the validated model, these cooperation-focused strategies are tailored to support users in collaboratively completing shared tasks and facilitate joint decisions, while incorporating varying degrees of turn-taking behaviour. For this, a hybrid architecture is proposed that integrates rule-based logic with data-driven techniques to represent domain-specific and domain-independent knowledge. The results of the conducted user studies show strong participant approval, with high ratings for both acceptance and efficiency of the agent. In this process, a dataset was collected that contains 40 conversations with a total of 1689 dialogue exchanges. In summary, this thesis presents a structured approach for enabling cooperative interaction in multi-user conversational agents. The findings of the experiments indicate that effective support in cooperative task-solving requires not only the continuous tracking and management of individual, group, and dialogue states, but also a deliberate application of dialogue strategies. By extending concepts of single-user dialogue management, the agent is equipped to handle the added complexities of multi-user interaction, including moderation, negotiation, and recommendation behaviour. This enables the system to provide more natural and context-aware assistance, constituting an important step towards conversational agents that function as intelligent collaborators in group environments.
