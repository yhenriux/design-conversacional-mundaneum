## Resumo disponível

This study aims to determine the human-likeness design combinations that would characterize a chatbot as human-like and non-human-like. The factors considered in the study are as follows: visual cue, identity cue, and conversational cue. Visual cue pertains to the chatbot avatar displayed in the chat, while identity cue pertains to how the chatbot introduces itself. Conversational cue focuses on the manner of the chatbot imitating human communication. Participants were tasked to rate a total of 8 design combinations from 1 (not human-like) to 5 (very human-like). To analyze the data gathered, Minitab tools such as full factorial design, ANOVA, and response optimization technique (with a 95% confidence level) were utilized. Based on the results, a high human-likeness chatbot should include the following characteristics: (1) human avatar, (2) introduces herself as a sales associate, and (3) acknowledges and sympathizes with the customer's previous message whereas a low human-likeness chatbot should have (1) a human avatar, (2) introduces herself as an automated customer service chatbot, and (3) acknowledges and sympathizes with the customer's previous message.
