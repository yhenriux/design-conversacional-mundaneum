## Resumo disponível

Conversational User Interfaces (CUIs) are gaining attention for improving human-machine interactions, offering support in areas like emotional assistance and customer care. Advancing CUIs requires a user-centered, collaborative approach to ensure they meet the needs of users, professionals, and companies, addressing both technical and user-centered challenges. This objective of the review is to synthesise scholarly works on co-creation methods, and undertake a SWOT analysis. Using search and inclusion/exclusion criteria based on the Population, Concept, and Context (PCC) framework, three information sources (Web of Science, ScienceDirect, and BMC Digital Health) are searched, and the PRISMA guidelines are followed to report the procedures and findings. Research questions to be addressed are; 1. How do developers determine which human participants (e.g., end-users, designers, developers) and tools (e.g. genAI tools such as ChatGPT) should be involved in the chatbot co-creation, requirements elicitation and stakeholder engagement process, and how do they recruit these participants or select these technologies? 2. What methods are used to gather requirements from both stakeholders and even AI technologies during the co-creation of chatbots? 3. How and when is feedback from stakeholders (e.g., end-users, designers, developers) and AI technologies (e.g., genAI) incorporated into the design and evaluation of chatbots? 4. In which elements of chatbot design and development is stakeholder feedback typically sought? 5. To what extent do co-creation, requirements elicitation and stakeholder engagement methods vary across different sectors/domains in the design of chatbots? 6. What terminology and theoretical frameworks are used to describe and guide the use of these methods in the design of chatbots? 7. What are the strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats (SWOT) associated with these methods for co-creating chatbots? The results will be discussed using a SWOT analysis, aiming to develop Best Practice Guidelines (BPG) to assist developers and designers in gathering and defining stakeholder-driven, humanity-centric requirements for conversational user interfaces (CUIs).
