## Resumo disponível

<jats:p>This design case documents the transformation of a two-bedroom rental apartment into a curated multi-room installation developed as a primary professional workspace and designed in part through sustained collaboration with a conversational AI system. The designer is an instructional design professional who works permanently from home; the apartment is her full-time professional workplace. Over several months, she built the installation around a single argument: that consumption-of food, of music, of media formats-is cumulative and meaning-making, not passive. What distinguishes the case is how the collaboration was conducted and documented entirely through preserved text-based conversation, without institutional infrastructure or design software. Those logs are the primary data. They capture decisions, reasoning, iteration, and failure at the moment of making-not reconstructed afterward. The AI's contributions were generative, incomplete, and at times instructively wrong. Its spatial reasoning failures produced one of the installation's most successful spaces. Its naming contributions gave the designer vocabulary she still uses. The case presents five key design decisions with analysis, documents the methodology and its limitations, and argues that the conversational log is a new and undertheorized form of design documentation the field should take seriously. It also argues that the work-from-home environment is a legitimate design problem, and treating it as one produces spaces that are more deliberate, more coherent, and more responsive to the specific person who lives and works in them.</jats:p>
