## Resumo disponível

<jats:title>Abstract</jats:title> <jats:p>In this work, we investigate the role of generative AI agents as reflective partners in engineering design tasks. While generative AI has been widely used to synthesize solutions in engineering design, there are growing concerns regarding its negative impact on the designers’ critical thinking and reasoning skills. To address this, we introduce Con-Struct, an AI-enabled workflow that leverages large vision-language models (VLMs) to support users in expressing their intuition and reasoning through reflective questioning. Using structural design as our context, we implement an interactive 2D structure design tool where users iteratively refine designs with the AI agent supporting their reflective thinking process. Using Con-Struct as our experimental setup, we conducted a between-subjects study with 24 participants, comparing a control group (no AI) with an experimental group (AI-assisted). While both groups performed similarly in design and evaluation tasks, the experimental group exhibited more deliberate design behavior, reduced complexity, and lower extraneous cognitive load. We further conducted thematic analysis on the experimental group’s interaction with the AI agent to identify unique strategies and conversational topics. Our analysis of the qualitative feedback showed control participants requesting guidance during the design tasks, while experimental participants found the AI agent valuable for reasoning through design decisions. Finally, we discuss strategies for designing more effective reflective workflows to support problem-solving and critical thinking in engineering design.</jats:p>
