## Resumo disponível

BACKGROUND State tobacco quitlines provide cessation support and nicotine replacement therapy (NRT) to United States (US) residents. Although live phone coaching is the primary method of providing behavioral support, text messaging programs have been used as supplemental treatment in most US quitlines for more than a decade. Automated short message service (SMS) text messaging programs provide daily educational and motivational text messages. However, these programs cannot provide personalized messaging or respond to participant questions. OBJECTIVE The purpose of this pilot was to test the feasibility of delivering “Hope” a novel AI-based text messaging program to enrollees in a large state quitline. Hope is an AI-native SMS text-based conversational agent designed to provide highly personalized messaging and real-time responses to participant questions. METHODS The pilot study was conducted in November and December of 2025 with quitline enrollees in a large state who were offered Hope (described as an AI-based text messaging program). All those who enrolled in Hope received a 2-month follow-up survey. Primary outcomes were program feasibility and acceptability, measured by program acceptance rates (participants electing to use the Hope program), early opt out rates (participants stopping the program within the first two weeks), incidence of technical problems and engagement. Secondary outcomes included participant satisfaction as well as measures of trust and therapeutic alliance with the conversational agent. Data from a usual care (non-AI) quitline based text program, Text A Coach (TAC), from November and December 2024 was used as a reference. RESULTS Enrollment: 2933 tobacco users enrolled in the HOPE program during the pilot period which was 54% of quitline enrollees during that time (similar to the 52.6% acceptance rate for TAC in 2024). Opt-out rate within the first two weeks was 4.2% for Hope, and 13.3% for TAC (p <.001) and 4% of survey respondents reported technical problems with Hope. More participants engaged with Hope (63.9%) than with TAC (29.2%, p<.001). Hope program users had modest increases in quitline engagement, including higher rates of accepting NRT from the quitline than those in TAC (90.5% vs. 85.5%, ᵡ2=34.47, p<.001). Of the 2933 Hope enrollees, 810 completed the 2-month survey (27.6%). Overall participant satisfaction with Hope was rated as high, as was trust in, and therapeutic alliance with, the conversational agent. CONCLUSIONS Piloting an AI-based tobacco treatment texting program as an adjunct to tobacco quitline services was feasible with few technology difficulties and showed high acceptability. Hope users had higher engagement than the standard text program and modestly higher quitline engagement, including a higher rate of accepting NRT. Together this data suggests that in at least one text messaging application, artificial intelligence appeared to improve upon standard tobacco treatment.
