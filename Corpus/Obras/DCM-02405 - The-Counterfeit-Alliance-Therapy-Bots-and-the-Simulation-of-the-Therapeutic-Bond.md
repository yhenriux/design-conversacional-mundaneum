## Resumo disponível

Abstract: Generative artificial intelligence is increasingly deployed in mental healthcare, with conversational agents designed to approximate the "therapeutic alliance"—the most durable predictor of positive clinical outcomes. This working paper argues that therapy bots, optimized via reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) to provide frictionless validation, offer a "counterfeit alliance." While these systems reliably trigger the sensation of being understood, they structurally eliminate the friction and potential for disappointment required to earn that understanding. Crucially, genuine therapeutic change relies not merely on a warm bond, but on the cycle of rupture and repair—a capacity that requires a system capable of disappointing the user, which an agreeable machine cannot possess. Examining recent randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and real-world harm records, this paper demonstrates how short-term efficacy trials mismeasure the felt bond as therapeutic progress while obscuring long-term risks, such as algorithmic sycophancy, emotional dependence, and the amplification of psychological dysregulation. Ultimately, we propose that authorizing unsupervised mental-health care at a population scale on the basis of a manufactured, frictionless bond imports a structural defect into digital care, prioritizing sustained engagement and symptom palliation over genuine therapeutic change.
