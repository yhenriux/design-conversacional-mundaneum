## Resumo disponível

This dissertation investigates how the Repertory Grid Technique (RGT) can be partially automated through Hybrid User Interface (HUI) design. RGT is a structured yet open-ended qualitative method for eliciting personal constructs: the dimensions people use to distinguish between products, interfaces, places, services, or experiences. Although RGT is valuable for uncovering tacit subjective judgments, it is difficult to scale. A typical RGT study requires careful preparation, continuous facilitation during elicitation, and substantial interpretive work during analysis. These demands become especially challenging when RGT is deployed remotely or with larger participant samples. The central aim of this thesis is to reduce the time and labour burden of the RGT conduct phase while preserving the methodological qualities that make RGT useful. The thesis does not treat automation as a replacement for researchers. Instead, it argues for partial automation: routine orchestration, such as presenting triads, collecting constructs, supporting revisions, logging interaction traces, and capturing ratings, can be supported by an interface, while researchers remain responsible for study setup, methodological choices, and interpretation. The thesis develops and evaluates Q-Survey, a hybrid conversational–graphical platform for conducting RGT studies. In this work, a Hybrid User Interface refers to an interface that coordinates a graphical user interface with a conversational user interface. The graphical component supports visual inspection, element comparison, progress awareness, and rating input, while the conversational component guides participants through construct elicitation and clarification. This division of labour is important because RGT requires both structure and expression: participants need a clear workflow, but they also need space to formulate meanings in their own language. Methodologically, the dissertation follows a Research through Design approach. Across four iterative design loops, early chatbot prototypes are transformed into a more complete hybrid platform, later extended with large language model support and a researcher-facing Builder. Each loop contributes evidence to three main claims. First, hybrid interaction can make RGT-style elicitation more scalable by automating routine conduct tasks while preserving opportunities for participants to articulate and revise their constructs. Second, a Builder-based platform can make the RGT conduct workflow portable across studies, rather than requiring a bespoke implementation for every new experiment. Third, LLM-mediated scaffolding can provide useful design knowledge for qualitative elicitation, but only when its effects are made inspectable through structured prompts, revision support, and provenance-oriented logging. The empirical work begins with feasibility prototypes showing that conversational and graphical components can support remote RGT conduct. Subsequent evaluations examine usability, user experience, construct quality, and participant perceptions of the hybrid interface. The thesis then explores LLM-supported RGT conduct, including conversational strategies for prompting participants during construct elicitation. The findings suggest that cognitively oriented scaffolds, such as construct elaboration and contrast/reflection prompts, can encourage deeper construct articulation, whereas affective-only prompts may improve tone or motivation without necessarily increasing construct depth. These results show that LLM prompting is not neutral: conversational strategy shapes the kind of subjective data that participants produce. The later part of the thesis introduces Q-Survey Builder as a configurable platform for designing, deploying, and analysing RGT studies. Through the Builder, researchers can configure element sets, triad presentation, laddering prompts, rating procedures, and quality-screening rules. Platform-based replication studies in the domains of writing instruments and urban landscapes demonstrate that the same conduct workflow can be transferred across bounded domains. These studies also show the value of logging, manifest-based configuration, and provenance traces for making qualitative elicitation more transparent and auditable. The dissertation contributes to human–computer interaction, design research, and qualitative methodology in three ways. Theoretically, it reframes automation in qualitative research as a question of interface orchestration rather than simple replacement of human facilitation. Methodologically, it shows how RGT can be supported through a hybrid workflow that combines conversational elicitation with graphical structure and researcher oversight. Practically, it provides Q-Survey and Q-Survey Builder as research artefacts that demonstrate how subjective data collection can become more scalable, inspectable, and reusable. The thesis also identifies important limitations. The reported studies focus on selected domains and English-language deployments, and the platform should not be understood as a complete solution for all forms of RGT or qualitative interviewing. LLM-generated scaffolding introduces risks of steering, over-normalising, or subtly reshaping participant meaning. Automated clustering and summarisation can support analysis but cannot replace careful reading, open coding, and interpretive judgment. For this reason, the thesis argues for automation with human oversight, traceability, and methodological caution. Overall, this dissertation shows that RGT can be made more deployable through carefully designed hybrid interfaces. By shifting repetitive conduct tasks to the interface while keeping interpretive authority with the researcher, Q-Survey demonstrates a path toward scalable, transparent, and methodologically defensible subjective data collection. The broader contribution is a design-oriented account of how conversational AI, graphical interaction, and human-in-the-loop research infrastructure can work together to support qualitative insight without reducing it to fixed-choice survey logic or opaque automation.
