## Resumo disponível

<jats:p>Employees often withhold concerns, ideas, and critical observations at work, and even when they do speak up, their input may be filtered, discounted, or left without follow-up. Amid the broader rise of AI-enabled workplace technologies, conversational agents are increasingly being explored as channels for employee feedback. Yet design guidance is rarely grounded in the mechanisms that shape whether employees speak up and whether their input is likely to be heard. This thesis synthesizes 60 peer-reviewed journal articles in a design-oriented literature review to identify barriers and enablers of employee voice related to psychological safety and trust, examine how anonymity and perceived risk are treated in the literature, and derive design implications for conversational AI feedback systems. The review finds that fear of retaliation is the most pervasive barrier, appearing in 70 percent of the reviewed papers and spanning all eight mechanism clusters. Leader openness and consultation are the most frequent enablers, present in 48 percent of papers, but their e!ects depend on relationship quality and context. Voice e"cacy and futility emerge as a distinct pathway: employees who expect their input to be ignored remain silent even when they feel safe to speak up. Visible response and follow-up emerge as the most frequent design lever, accounting for 38 percent of the corpus, highlighting the importance of loop closure for sustaining voice. Anonymity is the least specified dimension, with 90 percent of papers failing to report anonymity conditions, limiting the transferability of prior findings to channels where identifiability can vary by design. Based on these patterns, five design principles are derived: close the loop visibly, signal trust and credibility, reduce perceived hierarchy, o!er configurable anonymity as a matter of degree, and frame feedback requests non-evaluatively. These principles translate mechanismlevel evidenceintoactionablepropositionsfordesigningconversational AI systemsthatfacilitate employee voice.</jats:p>
