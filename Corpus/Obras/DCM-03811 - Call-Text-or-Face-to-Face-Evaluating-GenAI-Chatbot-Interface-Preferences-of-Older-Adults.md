## Resumo disponível

As chatbots powered by artificial intelligence (AI) become increasingly integrated into the daily lives of older adults, it is essential to identify which interfaces best support their interaction preferences.Older adults are a diverse group with varying digital competencies and preferences.This research aims to inform the design of AI interfaces that accommodate this range of needs.We examine how three different chatbot interfaces, text-based, avatar-based, and call-based, affect older adults' perceptions of ease of use, accessibility, and reliability.Using a between-subjects experimental design, 101 participants were randomly assigned to interact with "Sally, " a generative AI (GenAI) chatbot, through one of the three interfaces, each delivering the same interaction flow.Analyses showed that the avatar-based interface yielded the highest perceived reliability, ease of use, and accessibility, based on changes in participants' pre-and post-survey responses.These findings suggest that avatar-based chatbots may offer a more trustworthy and user-friendly experience for older adults.
