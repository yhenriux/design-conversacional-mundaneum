## Resumo disponível

Artificial intelligence is increasingly used to support mental health, yet current chatbot prompts often follow a standardized, one-size-fits-all approach. This is particularly problematic for caregivers, whose emotional challenges are deeply shaped by personal, cultural, and caregiving contexts, and not being investigated well in the research. This study investigates how personalized, empathic chatbot prompt design can better support caregivers’ emotional regulation needs. Through co-design workshops and interviews with caregivers, mental health professionals, and HCI experts, we identify key barriers and design opportunities. Our findings highlight the importance of culturally responsive, emotionally attuned, and trust-enhancing interactions. Participants emphasized the need for adaptive prompts, sensitive emotional cue detection, and transparency in AI systems. We propose design dimensions for developing AI prompts that move beyond generic suggestions and a more context-aware support based on the user’s lived experience.
