## Resumo disponível

With advancements in speech recognition and natural language processing, voice assistants (VAs) now perform complex tasks, including serving as information sources. As VAs become increasingly embedded in everyday life, understanding how their conversational nature influences user perceptions is essential. This article investigates how social role (servant vs. partner) and speech style (assertive vs. affiliative, only in Experiment 1) shape perceived credibility and intelligence in VAs. Two preregistered vignette-based online experiments using a between-subjects design were conducted (NExperiment1 = 353 and NExperiment2 = 216). Speech style showed no main effect on perceived credibility or intelligence. However, pooled Bayesian data analyses revealed moderate evidence that social role significantly affected perceived credibility and stronger evidence for effects on perceived intelligence. Specifically, participants rated VAs in the partner role as more credible and intelligent than those in the servant role. These findings offer insights into how VA characteristics shape users perceptions.
