## Resumo disponível

The rapid proliferation of artificial intelligence (AI) technologies—including machine learning algorithms, natural language processing tools, and AI-powered chatbots—has begun to reshape the landscape of psychological practice, raising both opportunities and concerns for the profession. This paper presents a secondary, desk-based review synthesizing existing literature on the integration of AI into psychological assessment, diagnosis, intervention, and administrative practice. Drawing on peer-reviewed journal articles, professional guidelines, and policy documents published between 2018 and 2025, the review examines three central themes: the emerging roles AI tools play in augmenting psychologists’ clinical and research functions; the competencies psychologists require to ethically and effectively engage with AI-driven technologies; and the conceptual, ethical, and practical challenges associated with their adoption, including concerns regarding data privacy, algorithmic bias, the therapeutic alliance, and professional accountability. Findings suggest that while AI holds considerable promise for enhancing diagnostic accuracy, expanding access to mental health services, and reducing clinician workload, its integration is hindered by gaps in psychologists’ digital literacy, inconsistent regulatory frameworks, and unresolved questions about the preservation of human centered care. The review concludes by identifying priority areas for training curricula, ethical guideline development, and future empirical research. This synthesis contributes to ongoing professional discourse by consolidating fragmented evidence into a coherent framework for understanding AI’s evolving role in psychological practice.
