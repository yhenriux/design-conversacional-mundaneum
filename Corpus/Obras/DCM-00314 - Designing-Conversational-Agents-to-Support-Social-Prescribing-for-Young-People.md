## Resumo disponível

Mental health challenges among young people are a significant global concern, yet traditional services often struggle to support them effectively. In response, there has been growing interest in innovative approaches. Social prescribing, which connects individuals with community resources to promote well-being, offers a promising alternative. However, its implementation faces several barriers around engaging young people and supporting the role of link workers. In parallel, digital technologies are opening new opportunities in mental healthcare. Among these, conversational agents are valued for their natural language interaction and ability to provide accessible, scalable support across a range of functions. In this context, this study explores how conversational agents might be integrated into the social prescribing process. Through participatory design workshops with young people and link workers, it investigates current barriers and stakeholder expectations. Grounded in the lived experiences of both users and practitioners, this research seeks to inform the design of digital tool that support more effective models of social prescribing for young people.
