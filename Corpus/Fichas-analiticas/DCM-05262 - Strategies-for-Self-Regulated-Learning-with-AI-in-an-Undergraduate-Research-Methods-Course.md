## Resumo disponível

Undergraduate research methods education faces numerous challenges with respect to student affect, interest, and conceptual understanding.We incorporated a set of artificial intelligence chatbots into an introductory psychology research methods course, designed to promote students' self-regulation while learning to design their own research studies.Analyses of student-AI interactions and student focus group interviews revealed instances in which students used AI to exercise agency over their learning, deepening their understanding of a topic, and engaging in self-regulation.Findings also revealed instances of students' overreliance on AI.This study has implications for the design of AI chatbots to support research methods education, and self-regulated learning more broadly.Recent developments in artificial intelligence (AI) chatbots present new opportunities for supporting SRL in research methods courses.With their ability to interpret and assess students' behaviors and thinking, and provide appropriate scaffolding (Hilpert et al., 2023;Molenaar, 2022), AI tools can complement human instruction, and personalize learning (Mohebi, 2024).Students, for example, may ask a chatbot to help evaluate their understanding, to adapt its level of complexity, and to redirect its focus away from, or toward topics that students feel they need more or less practice.However, we recognize the potential for students to overly rely on the chatbot at the expense of developing agency.Indeed, research on AI-supported learning, specifically on ChatGPT-supported learning (Lo et al., 2024), suggests mixed effects on student outcomes.With these trade-offs in mind, this study asks: In what ways do students demonstrate SRL with an AI chatbot designed to support their learning of experimental research?Answers to this question will inform how to design and integrate AI into research methods curriculum in ways that enrich students' learning experiences.
