## Resumo disponível

This study presents the impact of a collaborative conversational agent on the chat dialogues of 17 dyads.Specifically, participants were asked to study two cases of climate change using the chat tool of a learning platform to communicate.The agent was available only in the second case and was able to monitor the participants' discussion and intervene using the talk moves of the academically productive talk framework (e.g., prompting for elaboration, examples, etc.).Analysis of the chat logs showed higher levels of elaboration for the second case, while the agent acted as a catalyst increasing the chances for elaboration and transactivity. Conversational agents and productivity goals in collaborative learningStudies in Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning (CSCL) have shown that students can be more engaged during the lessons in online learning environments structured for intense exchange of ideas (Eshuis et al., 2019;Nguyen, 2023).Particularly in small groups, students have more time to verbalize these ideas, and therefore more opportunities to engage in productive student dialogue (Hu & Chen, 2023).Yet, productive dialogue does not happen spontaneously (Michaels & O'Connor, 2015).Consequently, students spend most of the time with their peers addressing each other's confusion and progressing on their own.While this can encourage autonomy, the lack of guidance can lead to slower progress or gaming-the-system behaviors that hinder learning (de Jong et al., 2023).Therefore, guidance is needed, and teachers must follow groups to provide adequate guidance (Howe, 2014).The Academically Productive Talk (APT) framework aims to help teachers support their students in reaching four key goals of productive dialogue (Michaels & O'Connor, 2015):• G1: Helping students share their reasoning;• G2: Helping students orient and listen to one another;• G3: Helping students deepen their reasoning; and• G4: Helping students engage with each other's reasoning.
