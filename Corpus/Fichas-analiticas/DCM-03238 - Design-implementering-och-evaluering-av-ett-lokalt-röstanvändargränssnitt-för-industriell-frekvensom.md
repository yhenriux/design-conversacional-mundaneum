## Resumo disponível

Voice-based interaction presents a compelling opportunity to complement touch-based interfaces in industrial mobile contexts, particularly in field environments where hands-free and eyes-free operation is advantageous. This thesis investigates the design, implementation, and evaluation of an on-device voice user interface (VUI) framework for ABB's Drive Composer application, prototyped in a mobile context. The framework enables voice-based interaction with variable speed drives, supporting tasks such as querying and modifying drive parameters that would otherwise require manual navigation through complex interfaces. The research follows a Design Science Research methodology across two iterative build-and-evaluate cycles. The interaction design is grounded in established VUI literature and addresses discoverability, cognitive load, and operational safety through multimodal visual and auditory feedback that supports user awareness and control. The framework adopts a fully on-device architecture to address industrial privacy, latency, and offline operation considerations, with intent recognition implemented using a deterministic rule-based approach that prioritizes predictability over linguistic flexibility. The summative evaluation combined quantitative technical performance measurement with a structured expert assessment of the prototype's usability characteristics, conducted across seven predefined interaction scenarios by a panel of domain experts, designers, and developers. The prototype achieved an overall command interpretation accuracy of approximately 96% and a mean processing latencyof approximately 42 ms, with usability ratings averaging 4.4 out of 5.0 across fiveVUI-specific criteria. The results indicate that a constrained rule-based architecturecan provide a technically feasible basis for implementing on-device voice interaction in an industrial drive control context. The VUI framework, architectural decisions, and evaluation findings provide documented design insights and implementation considerations that may inform future VUI development in comparable industrial contexts.
