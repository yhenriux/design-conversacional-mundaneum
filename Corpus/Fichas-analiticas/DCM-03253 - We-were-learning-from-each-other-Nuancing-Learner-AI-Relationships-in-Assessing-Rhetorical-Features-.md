## Resumo disponível

Assessing the reliability of information is one among several potential uses of Large Language Models (LLM).However, little research documents its application for educational purposes.We developed an educational conversational agent (Aide) that aims to help learners identify rhetorical aspects of textual disinformation.In this paper, we document different traits that users attribute to Aide through verbs that they use to describe its actions (such as analyze, teach, and explain).We also describe what attributes users in our sample identify as markers of reliability in Aide's analysis of text (such as reference to data and technical vocabulary).Finally, we present a concise case study to highlight discrepancies between users' perception of learning outcomes derived from using Aide (perceived as effective) vis-à-vis their approach to evaluate news stories pre-and post-interaction with Aide (unaltered) We conclude with a reflection on implications for the design of technologies that employ conversational agents for instruction aimed at enhancing learners' ability to assess information.
