Research in design cognition shows that evaluative discussions rely on the alignment of differing mental models and the exchange of ratio- nale [43, 49]. In professional settings, critique often involves cross- functional participants—such as designers, engineers, and product managers—who contribute distinct evaluative criteria. The inter- play among these perspectives introduces productive tension that can reveal blind spots and stimulate strategic trade-offs [7, 47, 58]. Rather than serving as passive inspectors, effective systems should facili- tate dialogic engagement and approximate the multi-perspective reasoning found in human teams.
